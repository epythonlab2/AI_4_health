What is Ethical AI?

Refers to the development and use of AI in ways that respect human values, rights, and societal well-being.

  • Treat people fairly
  • Protect privacy
  • Avoid harm
  • Remain accountable

Key Idea

AI should benefit society while minimizing risks.

Why Ethical AI Matters

Critical Decisions

Influences hiring, credit, healthcare, law enforcement, and education.

Social Protection

Poorly designed AI causes discrimination and privacy violations.

Harm Prevention

Prevents harmful decisions that can impact human lives permanently.

Examples of AI Risks

  • Algorithmic bias
  • Data misuse
  • Lack of transparency
  • Automation errors
  • Security vulnerabilities
Strategy

Responsible AI

Aims to identify and reduce these systemic risks before they affect users.

Fairness in AI

AI systems should treat all individuals and groups equally.

Challenges:

  • Biased training data
  • Unbalanced datasets
  • Historical discrimination

Example

A hiring algorithm that favors one gender over another based on historical job data.

Sources of Bias in AI

Data Collection

Non-representative samples.

Data Labeling

Human prejudice in tags.

Feature Selection

Proxies for race or gender.

Model Training

Optimizing for flawed goals.

Note: If historical data is biased, the model will learn and reproduce it.

Mitigating Bias

  • Use diverse datasets
  • Audit models regularly
  • Apply fairness metrics
  • Use bias detection tools

GOAL

Equitable and inclusive models.

Transparency in AI

Users should understand:

  • How the system works
  • What data is used
  • How decisions are made

The "Black-Box" Problem

Opaque models make it difficult to explain or contest outcomes.

Explainable AI (XAI)

Techniques that help make complex models understandable to humans.

Feature Importance

Which variables mattered?

SHAP Values

Contribution scores per feature.

LIME Explanations

Local surrogate modeling.

Accountability in AI

Organizations must take responsibility for AI decisions.

Key Questions:

• Who built the model?
• Who approved it?
• Who monitors its impact?

AI systems should not operate without human oversight.

Privacy and Data Protection

  • Personal information
  • Sensitive data
  • User identities

Techniques

Data anonymization, encryption, and secure storage protocols.

Data Governance

Quality

Ensures high-quality datasets.

Ethics

Ethical data collection methods.

Compliance

Legal standards (GDPR, etc.).

Poor governance leads to biased or unreliable models.

Security Risks in AI

Common Attacks

Responsible AI requires proactive security practices to defend against malicious interference.

Human Oversight

AI should support human decision-making, not replace it.

  • Final accountability
